01The problem, and how I approached it

Spotle Movies is Wordle for movies: guess a film, get eight clues back, and win within ten guesses. The question: what's the single best opening guess?

Measuring "good"

To rank thousands of candidates, I needed a precise definition of "informative": check a guess against every other film in the pool, and see how much its clues narrow things down.

Expected remaining
How many films stay consistent with a guess's clues, on average. Lower is better — 3,000 means the guess told you nothing; 1 means it solved the puzzle.
Entropy, in bits
The same idea in information-theory terms. Each bit roughly halves the field. A useless clue is 0 bits; one that pins down a single film is about 11.6 bits (log₂ of 3,000).

Entropy is what lets very different clues — a genre match, a score band — be compared on one scale, which is what the next section does.

The pool: 3,000 titles pulled live from TMDB, sized to Spotle's own description of a "curated ~3,000 film library," and checked against 20 real Spotle answers from the past month — 20 for 20 matched. Full methodology at the bottom.

Genres alone beats Cast and Director combined, by about 16×.

Genre match narrows more than a simple count would suggest — "Western" rules out far more than "Drama," since westerns are rare and dramas aren't, and the model credits that exact distinction. Cast almost never overlaps: a guess's five billed actors are absent from the other 2,999 films the vast majority of the time, so an all-star cast mostly just says "not this film." Rated is a solid mid-tier clue — only six ratings exist, so a match or miss splits a meaningful chunk of the pool. Score edges out Year because it's shown as a precise 0–100 integer.

Optimizing for fewest total guesses

These rankings measure the first cut, not the whole game — a guess can leave a small set that's awkward to finish (everything sharing the same genre and year) versus a slightly bigger, easier-to-split one. To check, I simulated full games: greedily pick the best next guess within whatever's left, turn after turn, and average the total guesses needed to win. (A truly exhaustive optimal-play search — trying every possible sequence, the way solved-Wordle research does — is out of reach for 3,000 films here; greedy is the standard near-optimal approximation, run on the ~120 strongest 1-step candidates.)

05Method, validation & caveats

The pool is pulled live from TMDB: /discover/movie finds candidates by vote count, then /movie/{id}?append_to_response=credits,release_dates pulls genre, cast, crew, revenue, score, studio, and US certification for each in one call. For every candidate guess, I computed its feedback signature against all other films, grouped films with identical signatures, and scored the guess by expected films remaining and by entropy.

Pool validated against real answers. I pulled 20 actual Spotle Movies answers spanning June 23–July 21, 2026 from third-party answer-archive sites and checked them against this pool: 20 of 20 matched, from Vertigo (1958) to Anora (2024). One page also published Spotle's own stated clues for Moneyball — every field matched our data exactly, evidence Spotle draws from the same underlying TMDB data this analysis does.
Full list of approximations (for the skeptical reader)
  • The pool is sized to match Spotle's own "curated ~3,000 films" description via a vote-count threshold, and spot-checked against real answers above. A 20-answer sample can't rule out edge cases — it's evidence of a good proxy, not proof of an identical list.
  • Studio "sister label" groupings (Disney/Pixar/Marvel, Warner/New Line, etc.) are a hand-built approximation of the real parent-company graph; only 7 major conglomerates (40 individual studio labels) are mapped.
  • "Lead" vs. "supporting" is approximated from TMDB billing order (order 0 = lead), which may not exactly match how Spotle classifies roles.
  • The director "yellow" rule uses real co-director pairs mined from full crew credits — but it's still bounded by what's in this 3,000-film pool, not all of film history.
  • 1.4% of films (42 of 3,000) have no US certification on file in TMDB; these are treated as a distinct "no data" state rather than guessed at.
  • Score is displayed 0–100 as a whole integer (e.g. TMDB's 7.299 → "73"). "Exact match" (green) means the two displayed integers agree, not raw float equality.
  • The expected-total-guesses figures use a greedy continuation strategy, not a fully exhaustive search over every possible sequence of guesses — the latter is what real Wordle-solver research grinds through dedicated compute for. Greedy is a well-established near-optimal approximation, but true optimal-play numbers could differ slightly.
  • Genres are matched exactly (which specific genres overlap), not by count, in every number on this page and in the simulator.
The overall shape of the result — genre/score/year/box-office carry the opener, cast and director don't (on average) — is robust to these approximations, since it comes from the size of the pool relative to how many distinct values each attribute can take, not the fine details of any one rule.
